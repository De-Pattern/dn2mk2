Start here

RIFT is a second firmware for the Elektron Digitone II (OS 1.11), separate from the DN2 mk2 mod. It drops the Machinedrum and Monomachine engines and puts new synthesis machines in their place: FASCIA (physical percussion and tuned bodies), ORGAN (a pipe organ), SILT (material synthesis), LIGATURE (a patchable block synth), DIGIDRUM (digital drums), THOLET (a felt/electric piano with a grain cloud). It keeps the Digitakt II sample machines, FLEX, the +Drive library, the recorder, Direct Jump and parameter slides from the mod. Unofficial: not made or supported by Elektron.

Everything below works like the stock Digitone II unless it says otherwise: p-locks, LFOs, slides, kits and sounds, copy/paste, MIDI. Every RIFT machine control can be p-locked and slid; most are LFO destinations (the few that are not are said).

Install & recovery

  • Back up first, every time: the +Drive and your projects with Elektron Transfer.
  • Build the file in the DN2 Mod Patcher's RIFT section from your own stock files (Digitone II OS 1.11, Digitakt II OS 1.17, Machinedrum UW OS 1.63). It only lets you save a file that is byte for byte the cleared build. Flash it over USB like any OS update.
  • Keep the stock OS 1.11 file and a DIN MIDI cable for recovery (the patcher's Flash section). The start-up code and the updater are Elektron's own, untouched.
  • From the DN2 mk2 mod: Perc and Monomachine tracks open as FM Tone. Keep RIFT projects apart from the mod's and stock's: RIFT's machines do not sound on either.
  • OS 1.11 only. On 1.12 or later, flashing is a downgrade (see the patcher).

Using RIFT's machines

  • MACHINE SEL ([FUNC] + [SYN]): RIFT's machines come after Swarmer, then the sample machines.
  • Pages: [SYN] steps through a machine's pages (SYN1, SYN2 ...). Each page shows eight controls in two rows of four, laid out like the knobs A-D (top) and E-H (bottom). Each control has a small icon that moves as you turn it; a choice shows its word in a box.
  • Values show while you turn a knob, while you hold [SYN], or while you hold a trig: the trig's p-locked controls are highlighted, with their locked values.
  • Choices (word controls) step four detents a choice, never glide, and stay choices under LFOs and slides.
  • Shortcuts: [FUNC] + turn moves FASCIA's TUNE by octaves and snaps GEOM and SHIFT to 0. [TRK] + CLEAR ([FUNC] + [PLAY]) puts a machine back to its own start values.
  • Start values: each machine starts at a playable sound when chosen in MACHINE SEL (the tables below say where each control starts; bipolar controls are shown around 0).
  • Voices and CPU: RIFT's machines share the DSP with the stock ones. When it gets busy, the oldest notes' tails give way first, never the newest note or a held chord.

FASCIA

Physical and spectral percussion and tuned bodies. Two exciters (page 1) strike, blow, bow or scatter dust into one or two resonant bodies (page 2): membranes, plates, bars, bells, glass, tubes, strings. Then a spectral stage (page 3) shapes what rings, and page 4 shapes the onset and how the two bodies interact.

How to approach it: pick the body first (BODA), set its pitch (TUNE) and ring (DAMP); then the exciter (TYPA, HARD); then colour (MATL, POS, GEOM). Body B adds a second, related body: start with XMOD EXCITE and raise COUP. Pages 3 and 4 are for character: a little CHAR, CLIK for definition, VARY so repeated hits differ.

Recipes: marimba: BAR, STRIKE, HARD 50, MATL low, DAMP 40. Tubular bell: CHIME, HARD 90, DAMP 20. Bowed glass: GLASS, BOW, DEC high, HARD 40. Snare: MEMB + BUZZ high, CLIK SNAP. Steel drum: PLATE + WATR. Gong: BELL + BODB PLATE, XMOD TRADE, FDBK a little.

Concept: De-Pattern. Not LFO destinations: ATK, FDBK, VARY.

SYN1 EXCITERS

TYPA
Exciter A		Exciter A: what sets the bodies moving. STRIKE is a mallet (a short pulse; HARD makes it narrower and brighter). BLOW is a reed driven by breath against the body (HARD brightens the turbulence). BOW is a friction bow computed every sample (HARD is bow pressure, airy to scraping; DEC keeps the bow on). DUST is sparse random impulses (their density is DUST). PULSE is a sine burst with a pitch drop set by BEND. TZFM is through-zero FM (SIDE sets the modulator ratio).
STRIKE, BLOW, BOW, DUST, PULSE, TZFM
TYPB
Exciter B		Exciter B: a second exciter of the same six types, mixed with A by MIX. Use two different types for hybrids: STRIKE + BOW for a bowed vibe with a mallet attack, BLOW + DUST for a breathy rattle.
STRIKE, BLOW, BOW, DUST, PULSE, TZFM
HARD
Hardness		Hardness: mallet hardness for STRIKE (soft felt at 0, a hard tip at 127), bow pressure for BOW, turbulence brightness for BLOW. Velocity scales it too. Also sets how loud CLIK's transient is.
DUST
Dust		Dust: contact noise and grit. Random micro-impacts on STRIKE, rosin grit (random catches) on BOW, and the impulse density of the DUST exciter.
TZFM
Thru-Zero FM		Thru-zero FM: on the TZFM exciter, the FM index (the phase can run backwards: metallic, then clangorous). On any other exciter, a short TZFM burst at the attack, for a bright metallic edge on a strike.
BEND
Bend		Bend: a pitch glide that follows the body's own level, up to 7 semitones at full: the tension glide of a struck drum skin. Hard hits also add a 4 ms contact chirp. On PULSE it is the burst's pitch drop.
MIX
Exciter Mix		Exciter mix: A only at 0, B only at 127, both between.
DEC
Exciter Decay		Exciter decay: how long the exciter keeps exciting. Short for a strike, long for a bow or breath that keeps the body singing.

SYN2 RESONATORS

BODA
Body A		Body A, the main resonator (16 modes). MEMB a drum skin (Bessel ratios), PLATE a metal plate, BAR a free bar (marimba-like), VIBE a tuned vibraphone bar, CHIME a tubular chime (struck note in tune), BELL measured bell ratios, GLASS a wine glass, TUBE odd harmonics (closed pipe, clarinet-like), STRING plain harmonics.
MEMB, PLATE, BAR, VIBE, CHIME, BELL, GLASS, TUBE, STRING
BODB
Body B		Body B, a second resonator of the same types, or OFF. Its pitch is fixed by its type against Body A (TUBE and STRING 1:1, MEMB and CHIME 3:2, GLASS 4:3, PLATE, VIBE and BELL 2:1, BAR 2.756), so pairs are always in a musical relation. How B hears A is XMOD and COUP.
MEMB, PLATE, BAR, VIBE, CHIME, BELL, GLASS, TUBE, STRING, OFF
TUNE
Tune		Tune: the bodies' pitch around the note, in semitones (bipolar; 64 is the note). FUNC + turn jumps by octaves (0, 32, 64, 96, 127).
GEOM
Geometry		Geometry: stretches or squeezes the mode ratios (ratio to the power 0.6 .. 1.4): left is more harmonic and woody, right more inharmonic and metallic. 64 is the body as measured. FUNC + turn snaps to the centre.
MATL
Material		Material: how fast the high modes die against the low ones. Low: wood or skin, the highs fade fast. High: metal and glass, the highs ring as long as the fundamental.
POS
Position		Position: where the body is struck, edge to middle. Each mode is weighted by how much it moves there, so POS sweeps the overtone balance (a hollow centre hit to a bright edge hit).
COUP
Coupling		Coupling: how much of Body A goes into Body B, in the way XMOD chooses.
DAMP
Damping		Damping: how long the bodies ring (the T60 of every mode). Low rings long (FASCIA starts at 28, about 3 s), high is a dead, short thud.

SYN3 SPECTRAL

MORPH
Morph		Morph: moves each mode's level up the spectrum as the note rings, keeping the total level, so the timbre brightens or darkens over the tail without moving the pitch.
FORM
Formant		Formant: two vowel formants across a e i o u, after the bodies. Sweep it slowly or with an LFO for talking bells.
SHIFT
Freq Shift		Frequency shift: a single-sideband shift of up to +-2000 Hz, bipolar around 64 (0 Hz). Small amounts give slow phasing beats; large amounts make any body inharmonic and alien. FUNC + turn snaps to 0.
RING
Ring Mod		Ring mod: Body A times Body B, or times a sine at 1.414 x the note when Body B is OFF.
SIDE
Sideband		Sideband: the TZFM modulator's ratio (0.5 to 7): sets which sidebands TZFM makes.
FOLD
Fold		Fold: a sine wavefolder on the output. Gentle at first, then rich and buzzy.
LPG
Low Pass Gate		Low-pass gate: a contour that closes as the note dies, both darkening (it damps the high modes) and quietening it, the classic plucked/bongo decay.
CHAR
Character		Character: an asymmetric saturation (warmth, then grit). FASCIA starts at 127.

SYN4 ONSET/INTERACT

ATK
Attack		Attack: the onset. On strikes it smears the hit over a rising window (a swell); on BOW and BLOW it is the rise time of the pressure. 0 is instant.
CLIK
Click		Click: a transient straight to the output, of the type CTYP picks, scaled by HARD. Adds stick, tick or thump definition without changing the body.
CTYP
Click Type		Click type: SNAP a band-limited crack, TICK a 1 ms high blip, NOISE a short bright burst, THUMP a 40 Hz knock with a falling pitch.
SNAP, TICK, NOISE, THUMP
XMOD
Cross Mod		Cross mod: how Body A acts on Body B. EXCITE: B is also struck by A's sound (COUP of it). BEND: A's level bends B's pitch, up to an octave. CHOKE: A ringing shortens B. TRADE: energy flows both ways (B back into A, A damped by what it gives).
EXCITE, BEND, CHOKE, TRADE
FDBK
Feedback		Feedback: Body B back into Body A, softly limited. Low amounts thicken and sustain; high amounts self-oscillate into howls.
BUZZ
Buzz		Buzz: a contact gap on Body A. Where A swings past it, it buzzes (snare wires, a sitar's jawari, a rattle); the buzz also shakes Body B.
WATR
Water		Water: the sound vocodes itself above the note: its upper partials heard through narrow detuned bands, gurgling like a water drum or a waterphone. The fundamental stays clean.
VARY
Variation		Variation: per-hit randomness (a little mode tuning and level each strike) and a slow drift of the modes while they ring. Brings repeated hits to life.

ORGAN

A pipe organ from Ravel (the FM-1 firmware): five stops and seven drawbar ranks, with the pipes' chiff and speech, wind, tremulant and celeste (page 2), a percussive attack, mixture and rotor (page 3), and breath you can play like a flute (page 4). Every page 3 and 4 control at 0 is the plain organ.

How to approach it: page 1 is registration: choose a STOP, then pull drawbars. 8' alone is the plainest sound; add 4' and 2' for a principal chorus, 2 2/3' and 1 3/5' for the Cornet, 16' for weight. Page 2 makes it a real instrument: CHIFF and SPEECH for the attack, WIND and AIR for life, CELESTE for shimmer. The church is the Digitone's reverb: send it there.

Recipes: Plenum: PRINCPL, 16' 8' 4' 2' up, MIXT up. Vox celeste: GAMBA, 8' only, CELESTE up. Flute 4': FLUTE, 4' only, CHIFF 60. Played flute: FLUTE, BRTH 60, BLOM 50, EDGE 40, PVEL up. Drawbar organ: PERC 80, KCLK 50, ROTR 70.

Concept: De-Pattern (Ravel's organ). Not LFO destinations: BLOM, EDGE, PVEL.

STOP
Stop		Stop: the pipe family, which sets every rank's tone. FLUTE (pure, hooty), NACHTHN (Nachthorn: a soft wide flute), PRINCPL (Principal: the classic church organ), GAMBA (a thin, string-like stop), TRUMPET (a reed, bright and brassy).
FLUTE, NACHTHN, PRINCPL, GAMBA, TRUMPET
16FT
Rank 16 Foot		The 16' rank: an octave below the note. Each R control is a drawbar: its level in the mix.
8FT
Rank 8 Foot		The 8' rank: the note itself.
4FT
Rank 4 Foot		The 4' rank: an octave above.
NAZ
Nazard		Nazard (2 2/3'): the 12th, an octave and a fifth up. With 8' and 4', the hollow 'quint' colour.
2FT
Rank 2 Foot		The 2' rank: two octaves up.
TIER
Tierce		Tierce (1 3/5'): the 17th, two octaves and a third up. With the Nazard, the reedy Cornet colour.
1FT
Rank 1 Foot		The 1' rank: three octaves up. Sparkle on top.

SYN2 PIPES

CHIFF
Chiff		Chiff: the breathy 'puff' at the start of each pipe's speech. 0 is a clean onset.
SPCH
Speech		Speech: how slowly each pipe comes up to pitch and level. Low is immediate; high lets each note bloom.
SPRD
Spread		Spread: detunes the ranks slightly against each other, a large organ's natural chorus.
AIR
Air		Air: the noise of wind in the pipes, under the tone.
WIND
Wind		Wind: the wind supply's instability, a slight unsteady wobble of pitch and level, as a real bellows.
TREM
Tremulant		Tremulant: depth of the organ's tremulant (a pulsing of the wind).
RATE
Tremulant Rate		Tremulant rate.
CELST
Celeste		Celeste: adds a slightly sharp extra rank (the Voix Celeste) that beats against the rest, a slow shimmering chorus. Fades in and out smoothly.

SYN3 ATTACK

PERC
Percussion		Percussion: a Hammond-style sine ping on the 2nd harmonic at each strike. 0 is off.
PDEC
Percussion Decay		Percussion decay: 40 ms to 1.5 s.
MTUN
Mixture Tune		Mixture tune: tunes the mixture's two pipes down 0..36 semitones (they sit at 6 and 16 times the note at 0).
KCLK
Key Click		Key click: a 12 ms contact click at the strike.
MCHR
Mixture Character		Mixture character: morphs the mixture's pipes toward the TRUMPET's tone (toward the FLUTE's on a TRUMPET stop).
MIXT
Mixture		Mixture: the level of the mixture (high pipes at 6 and 16 times the note): a bright, glittering crown.
ROTR
Rotor		Rotor: a rotating speaker's depth: level and tone swirl, plus Doppler on the pipes.
RSPD
Rotor Speed		Rotor speed, with a 0.7 s lag when it changes (the horn spinning up and down).

SYN4 BREATH

BRTH
Breath		Breath: the jet's turbulence inside the tone: level, pitch flutter and a darkening. Turns the organ into a played flute.
TURB
Turbulence		Turbulence: the breath's rate, 1.5 to 59 Hz.
BREK
Break		Break: a downward voice-break, every other cycle changed, a component at half the pitch (overblown/underblown flute).
BDEP
Break Depth		Break depth: how strong the break is (up to 7 dB under the note).
PRES
Pressure		Pressure: the blowing pressure: sharper, and more likely to break.
BLOM
Bloom		Bloom: the pipe opens slowly from breath into tone (30 ms .. 3 s). With EDGE at 0 it is a pure swell.
EDGE
Edge		Edge: breath through the pipe, noise on a band-pass at the note. What you hear as BLOM opens.
PVEL
Pressure Velocity		Pressure velocity: velocity on the pressure (harder notes blow harder).

SILT

Material synthesis: four materials (FIBER, SILT, SKIN, FLUID) played by ten gestures, from a bow to a quake, by one player or an ensemble. You play it in three layers:

1. Choose on page 4 (per trig, p-lockable): what the matter is (MAT), what you do to it (GEST), how many players (MODE), how they are laid out and move (DIST).

2. Shape on pages 1-3: MATTER, CONTACT, STRUCTURE. Each knob is one physical idea that every material reads in its own way (PRESS is always 'how hard').

3. Play: velocity strikes harder and bows faster (and in MASS sets how many players join); the trig's length is how long the gesture is held; LOOP restarts it while held.

STRESS is a hidden macro, how near the matter is to failing, made from PRESS, ROSIN, INST, ROUGH and SCRAP. FIBER squeals and breaks up, SKIN stiffens and jumps, SILT avalanches, FLUID drips faster. It has a soft ceiling so it stays musical.

Pairs to explore: PRESS x ROSIN (stable to squeal); PRESS x SPEED (flautando to crushed); SPREAD x DRIFT x COHE (the cluster); SNAP x VISC on SKIN (pluck to thud); VISC x TENS x SPEED on FLUID (water to honey); GLISS x DIST x SHAPE (the glissando family); MODE MASS against MASS-8.

The start sound is a gently bowed FIBER string, in tune. Page 4 is p-lockable per trig.

Concept: De-Pattern. Not LFO destinations: SHAPE, TUNE, QUAL.

LIGATURE

A patchable synth voice built from twelve small blocks, after WEFT (the Ravel FM-1's block voice). Each block does one thing (an oscillator, an envelope, a filter, a VCA, logic, a resonator) to its two inputs, A and B, using two settings. You wire blocks into each other and choose which block is the output. The same voice plays every note of the track.

Each project keeps 128 wirings. PATCH (page 1, 1..128) picks which one the track plays, and is p-lockable (a different patch per trig). A new project's wirings start as the 13 factory patches (slots 1-13), then empty INITs.

Page 1: the PATCH screen

The top of page 1 shows the wiring as a grid of twelve tiles, in WEFT's order: 1-9, then A, B, C (blocks 10-12). Each tile names its block's type. Cables in the gutters show what feeds what; a cable back to an earlier block (feedback) is dotted. The output block is marked. Below the grid are page 1's eight controls in their knobs' places.

  • [YES] opens EDIT on the selected block:
  • A BLK picks the block (or use the arrows to move between tiles).
  • B TYP its type (the 22 blocks below, or empty).
  • C INA what feeds input A; D AMA how much (-100..+100 %; negative inverts).
  • E INB what feeds input B; F AMB how much.
  • G MOV (B MOVES): instead of feeding the block's B input, B moves the block's second setting (once a block, 667 times a second): use it to modulate a setting the block has no input for.
  • H OUT: which block the voice plays (the output).
  • [FUNC] + [NO] empties the block. [YES] again shows the BLOCK view: its inputs, its two settings and where it goes. [NO] leaves EDIT.
  • Every change is heard at once and kept in the project (saved with it).

Pages 2-4: the settings

Each block has two settings; pages 2-4 hold them: page 2 blocks 1-4, page 3 blocks 5-8, page 4 blocks 9-12, two knobs a block. Each knob is named for its block and setting (for example 1RISE, 3CUT). They are offsets from the wiring's own setting: 0 is the patch as wired, so you can p-lock, slide and LFO them around it, and a PATCH change still sounds as designed. A knob whose block is empty does nothing.

Signals are roughly -1..+1 (audio) or 0..1 (envelopes, gates). Any output can feed any input, audio or control: that is the fun of it.

Patching: worked examples

  • A basic voice (INIT): 1 WAVE (free-running), 2 SLOPE (envelope), 3 LP (A from 1, B from 2: the envelope opens the filter), 4 MULT (A from 3, B from 2: the VCA). OUT = 4.
  • FM: 1 PHASE (RATI 2), 2 WAVE (A from 1: a sine at 2x), 3 PHASE (A from 2, AMA 30 %: 2 modulates its rate), 4 WAVE (A from 3), 5 SLOPE, 6 MULT (A 4, B 5). OUT = 6. Raise AMA on block 3 for more brightness; put a SLOPE into it for a decaying FM index.
  • A plucked drum (TOMSKIN): 1 SLOPE with A = TRIG (a fast hit), 2 SKIN (A from 1; B from VEL so harder hits are higher), 3 SAT. Change 2 to BAR for a marimba.
  • Slope pinging a skin, looping: 1 CYCLE (RATE to taste) feeds 2 SLOPE's A (it retriggers at each cycle), 2 into 3 SKIN's A. OUT = 3: a self-playing hand drum. Put LFO1 into the SKIN's B for a pitch wobble.
  • Random melodies: 1 CYCLE (the clock), 2 HOLD (A = NOIS, B from 1, STEP up to quantise), 3 PHASE (A from 2: the held value moves its pitch), 4 WAVE (A from 3), 5 MULT (A 4, B = GATE). OUT = 5.
  • Feedback: wire a later block into an earlier block's input (e.g. 4 into 1's A). The return is one block (0.67 ms) late; small amounts thicken, large ones scream. Keep a SAT or a filter in the loop.
  • Use G MOV when a block's second setting has no input: e.g. on a FOLD, MOV with B = LFO1 sweeps OFFS; on a RUNG, MOV moves LEN.

Concept: De-Pattern (WEFT, from Ravel). Not LFO destinations: 11B, 12A, 12B.

THOLET

A piano that leans toward a Rhodes: one of six real grand-piano hammer transients starts each note, two detuned sets of twelve string partials ring under it, a felt layer softens it after Nils Frahm, EP morphs it toward an electric piano, and a grain cloud (page 2) lets the note break up into grains as it fades.

How to approach it: pick a HAMR and set DEC; FELT for the felt piano, EP for a Rhodes, STRK for warmth. On page 2, raise GRAN and the tail turns into a cloud: GDEN, GSIZ, GSPR shape it, GFM makes it waver. REL is the damper when a note ends (use the trig's length, or HOLD).

Concept: Error Kit. Hammer samples: Kawai grand (VCSL, CC0).

Sample machines

ONESHOT, WERP, STRETCH, REPITCH, GRID, SLICE and FLEX, from the Digitakt II, play samples from the project's sample list and the +Drive library (16,384 samples, in folders).

  • SAMP picks a sample; turning past 127 reaches the eight FLEX buffers B1..B8 ("FLEX BUF n" when one holds a take): any sample machine can play a FLEX take.
  • EDIT SLICE POINTS (SLICE, GRID): edits are heard 0.2 s after the knobs rest; the slice that plays is selected; ZOOM shows 128 frames of the sample itself; [FUNC] + START / END snaps to the nearest zero crossing.
  • CREATE SLICE GRID: [FUNC] + E jumps SLICES 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 / 64.
  • GRID's ALLOCATE SLICE: a lock per trig; [NO] keeps RANDOM's locks.
  • The +Drive library: nested folders, a file browser with marks, SELECT UNUSED, the stock NAME screen for file names.

FLEX

FLEX is a live-buffer machine like the Octatrack's Flex: it records into one of eight buffers (B1..B8) and plays them back with ONESHOT's controls (BUF in SAMP's place). Every sample machine can also play a buffer.

  • Sources (REC SETUP's SRC): TRK1..16 (one track's own sound, before its level and sends: a bounce), IN (the external inputs), MAIN (the whole mix as heard, effects included; a FLEX track sounding is recorded too, the Octatrack way: mute it, or record with TRK, for a clean bounce).
  • Three ways to record: recorder trigs on their step for R.LEN steps (a one-shot trig records once, then is spent; [YES] [YES] re-arms); Q.REC, trigless: [YES] while playing waits for the next boundary (OFF, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 steps, or the pattern length); and the recorder's own ARM / R.STRT.
  • R.LEN counts steps at the pattern's scale; [FUNC] + turn moves it a bar (16 steps) a detent.
  • MUTE SRC: the source track is silenced in the mix while the FLEX track plays it back (held 0.2 s after the note).
  • Level: a take of a track plays about as loud as the track at GAIN 0. A take of MAIN or IN is recorded as it comes in (never pushed into clipping) and played back 12 dB louder (v0.14). GAIN adds to that.
  • Saving ([FUNC] + [YES]): the take goes into the next empty sample slot and onto the +Drive, not normalised. (A saved MAIN or IN take plays 12 dB quieter than the buffer did: the save keeps the take as recorded.)
  • Switching a track to FLEX sets its trig length to INF, so you hear the whole take. A BUF lock on a trig plays that buffer.

Recorder

  • The recorder (the DN2 mk2 mod's) records IN, MAIN or a track into the sample list and the +Drive, with threshold arming, R.STRT on the transport and trimming.
  • MON (v0.14): knob C on the RECORDER turns monitoring on: with SRC IN, the input is heard through the main out while the recorder is armed or recording, at the level it records. "MON" shows in the corner while it is on. It starts OFF and is not saved.

Microtuning

  • One tuning per project, and each of the 16 tracks tuned or not (v0.14).
  • 11 factory tunings: 12-TET, Just Major, Just Minor, Pythagorean, Meantone 1/4, Werckmeister III, Kirnberger III, Vallotti, Arabic Rast, Poole Rod12, Poole12 Min; and USER 1..8.
  • KB SETUP's H slot is TUNE: turn it to set the track's tuning; push it to open TUNING (A TUNE, B ROOT, C TRK on/off, D ALL tracks).
  • [YES] on a USER tuning opens EDIT: A NOTE, B CENT, C .1CT (tenths of a cent), D FROM (a tuning to copy; [FUNC] + [YES] copies it in).
  • Everything a tuned track plays is retuned: keyboard, trigs, chords, arp and slides, on every RIFT machine. MIDI tracks and MIDI out stay 12-TET; SLICE and GRID are never retuned. Saved with the project.

Performance

  • Direct Jump: hold [PTN] + [YES] turns it on (DIRECT JUMP ON), [PTN] + [NO] off. A pattern cued with [PTN] + [TRIG] then starts at once, from where the playing pattern was. Kept through power-off.
  • Parameter slides (Overtone / A4 mk2 style): on the TRIG page's second page, SLD (E) and SLEN (F). A trig with SLD on glides every continuous p-lock from the track's value to the lock over SLEN steps. Works on every RIFT machine control.
  • KEYBOARD HOLD (v0.15): hold [PTN] and press [KEYBOARD]: KB HOLD ON (again: OFF). It is the stock sustain pedal on all 16 tracks: notes already held stay on when let go, new notes held join them, MIDI notes in and the arpeggiator's keys are held too. HOLD OFF releases them all. The sequencer's notes are not held.
